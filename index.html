Hypothesis Part 1 (ML Top-K > Single Centralities): Rejected. Under equal budget (k = 10, p = 0.05), PageRank (55.16%), Betweenness (54.51%), and Degree (53.40%) all statistically significantly outperformed raw AI top-K (47.62%, paired t-test p < 10−35).

The Audience Overlap Paradox: Why Centrality Beat Machine Learning

A critical distinction between single-node prediction precision and multi-node collective cascade efficiency.

1. The ML Model Was Extremely Accurate

Random Forest achieved an R2 = 0.9705 and Spearman ρ = 0.9788 in predicting single-node diffusion potential. Feature importance proved that core_number (k-core decomposition) overwhelmingly dictates individual reach (87.3% impurity importance).

2. High-Core Nodes Cluster Together

Because high k-core nodes live within dense internal subgraphs, selecting the top-10 individually highest predicted nodes clustered 6 out of 10 seeds inside Community 9 alone. Their cascades immediately collided, causing severe audience redundancy.

3. PageRank Spanned Communities Naturally

PageRank seeds naturally dispersed across 8 separate communities. By initiating spread from multiple distinct topological sectors, PageRank avoided audience cannibalization and achieved peak adoption velocity (675.6 nodes/step).

Research Methodology & Mathematical Framework

Formal specifications for diffusion models, feature extraction, ML training, and community optimization.

1. Diffusion Simulation Mechanics

Independent Cascade (IC) Model: At step t, each newly activated node u receives a single chance to activate each currently inactive neighbor v across edge (u, v) with independent propagation probability p = 0.05. If successful, v becomes active at step t + 1. The cascade terminates when no new activations occur.

Linear Threshold (LT) Model: Each node v draws an internal adoption threshold θv ∼ U(0, 1) representing its resistance to adoption. Incoming edge weights are normalized by node degree wuv = 1 / d(v). A node v activates at step t + 1 if and only if the cumulative influence of its active neighbors meets or exceeds its threshold:
∑u ∈ N(v) ∩ At wuv ≥ θv   (where θv ∼ U(0, 1), wuv = 1 / d(v))

2. SNA Feature Extraction & ML Regression Pipeline

For all 4,039 nodes, 10 topological features were computed: degree, degree centrality, betweenness, closeness, pagerank, eigenvector centrality, clustering coefficient, average neighbor degree, core number (k-core decomposition), and community size.

Ground truth labels were generated by simulating 50 independent single-node Monte Carlo cascades at p = 0.05 for 1,500 sampled candidate nodes. The dataset was split into 80% train and 20% test (N = 808). Random Forest Regressor (100 estimators, random state 42) was trained to predict expected single-node cascade size Ŝ(v).

3. Greedy Community-Aware Seed Optimization Algorithm

To counteract the audience overlap problem, candidate seeds were selected iteratively from a pool of the top 300 ML-ranked candidates using a penalized greedy objective:
v* = argmaxv ∈ P \ S [ Ŝ(v) − β • count(comm(v) ∈ S) − γ • overlap_penalty(v, S) ]

With parameters α = 0.6, β = 0.25, γ = 0.5. This penalty directly prevented over-allocation of marketing spend to a single community, expanding represented communities from 3 to 6 and boosting coverage by +1.89 percentage points.
